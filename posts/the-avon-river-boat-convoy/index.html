The Avon River Boat Convoy

September 21, 2026

This was originally published in the 2026 Canta Te Taiao Issue, not much deviates from here apart from higher quality and different photos.

I decided I was going to go down the Avon in an inflatable boat yet again, but this time, with more people than just my co-pilot and occasional squash game partner Marten.

Using my absolute wizardry as a wannabe graphic designer, I whipped up a poster of similar quality to the Mona Lisa and slapped 100 of them into bathroom stalls and random corners around campus. The QR code on it lead to a Facebook event, which steadily grew to over 110 people flirting with the idea. 

A couple weeks had passed and the day had finally come. Parking at the final destination, I took my semi-deflated boat out of the car and, while juggling the oars in one hand, biked my way to the starting carpark in Fendalton. With the event due to start in 15 minutes, I was a little bit worried that it would just be me and my neighbours who were rabidly keen for the idea, but oh boy was I wrong.

About five minutes before the start time, a red Skoda pulls up, with four rando's stumbling out, one of them exclaiming "WHOS READY TO CONVOY DOWN THE AVON”.

After that, I knew this was going to be absolutely baller. 

Exchanging names that I’d quickly forget, the carpark slowly filled up, similar to a bus on a compulsory 8am fieldtrip, with most people coming well after we were due to start. From tractor tubes to warehouse inflatables, queen sized air mattresses and a restored row boat, the variety of characters and boats were dumbfoundingly beautiful. The fattest grin wrapped my face, my stupid ideas do come to fruition, sometimes.

Consisting of a decent quarter of the group, the inflatable mattress crew leapt ahead, not necessarily with consultation from the rest of the group, but we all set off nevertheless. For the first half hour, we meandered beside the backyards of billion dollar houses.

We passed the bustling Riccarton Bush market. My co-captain Marten and I quickly parked up to fill the boat with air (this would be a common occurrence). Meanwhile, my co-pilot quickly fueled up with an overpriced local delicacy

This pitstop was useful for the tail end of the convoy to catch up. At this point, there was a solid five minute gap between the air mattress front runners and us. As we sailed away, an old man dashes in our direction and offers a lamington for disposal, for which I gladly consume.

After our picnic excursion, we manage to catch up to the front runners, who were slowing down for some reason other than being kind. I am mildly curious as to why they are yelling out to me.
Turns out my wonderful research didn't determine if there were any "difficult" sections along the route (I was lazy and it was cold the night before so my planned scouting did not occur)

I am mildly curious as to why they are yelling out to me.

This “difficult” section was infact a metre and half drop with a bridge over it. That doesn't seem scary until you’re on an inflatable boat. With the wooden boat coming downstream, we knew it would end up being more useful as firewood if it went through the levee. We carried the boat underneath the bridge while our legs half froze. My co-pilot did not want to attempt the levee rapids, but that didn't stop me from trying. With the amount of splashing that occured, I knew that I needed to fully decontaminate in the shower once I got home.

After this mild speed bump, we commenced our cruise down the Avon. Discussing with fellow boaters, we found out that one crew consisted of Dunedin students. How boring does Dunedin have to be that you drive five hours to Christchurch for some (albeit epic) boating.

We said goodbye to some of the more temperamental boats at Mona Vale and continued through the second rapids. Once again, it was a team effort to lift the wooden boat down. 

Passing by Hagley Park, we found an old disused swing which we pulled out of a tree using an oar. Some people were brave enough for a dip, but I was more than fine swinging around in circles. The majority of people left after this. It was already midday, and IDK I suppose people have assignments and lives and stuff. 

The rest of the trip consisted of repumping my leaking boat (remediating punctures with more duct-tape), and getting towed by the rowboat as we were all getting quite tired. The captain of a pink flamingo with it’s wing ripped off was shivering and I suggested that he hoped in my boat instead of being half submerged in the Avon.

He climbed aboard. 

Towards the end, we passed by some kayakers and Punters on the Avon™, who were clearly jealous and intimidated of our boats. We did try to trade our boats with the punters but to no luck. I believe it was a missed opportunity to have a piece of Christchurch history.

After (a mildly grueling) five hours, we finally pulled up at Riverside Market, which while not being our original final destination, was a more than welcome finishing line. Hugs, photos, and cheers were shared around as we shivered in the finishing sunlight.

Next year (and believe me, there will be a next year), I will make it much shorter, finishing at the swing, and I actually will print out my certificates for coolest boats. I was really lazy and rushed so I didn't print them out in time.

Long story short, it was baller, do more silly things, organise with friends, remember university isn't just locking in, and write something interesting.
